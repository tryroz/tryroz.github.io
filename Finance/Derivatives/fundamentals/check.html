G⁢Mr2=1r⁢(2⁢π⁢rP)2=4⁢π2⁢rP2.

Thus:

r3P2=G⁢M4⁢π2.

Question 2

(a) Momentum conservation check

If the initial momentum is zero, then the final total momentum must also be zero:

p→i=p→f=0.

So if a proposed explosion has a piece moving in one direction without another piece carrying equal and opposite momentum, the scenario is impossible. It would violate conservation of momentum.

(b) Two fragments after an explosion

Suppose the object starts from rest and breaks into two pieces with masses 3⁢m and m. Let the lighter piece move with speed v. Let the heavier piece have velocity v1.

By conservation of momentum:

0=(3⁢m)⁢v1+m⁢v.

Cancel m:

3⁢v1+v=0.

So

v1=−v3.

The minus sign means the heavier piece moves in the opposite direction.

The kinetic energy of the heavier piece is

K1=12⁢(3⁢m)⁢(v3)2=16⁢m⁢v2.

The kinetic energy of the lighter piece is

K2=12⁢m⁢v2.

Thus

K1K2=16⁢m⁢v212⁢m⁢v2=13.
v1=−v3,K1K2=13.

Question 5

A spaceman travels outward at

v1=13⁢c

and returns home at

v2=23⁢c.

On Earth, the total elapsed time is 9 years. Use units where

c=1⁢ly/yr.

Let the outward trip take t1 years in the Earth frame. Then the return trip takes 9−t1 years.

The distance out must equal the distance back:

13⁢t1=23⁢(9−t1).

Multiply by 3:

t1=2⁢(9−t1).

So

t1=18−2⁢t1,3⁢t1=18,t1=6⁢yr.

The return trip takes

9−6=3⁢yr.

The turnaround distance is

x=13⁢(6)=2⁢ly.

Now compute the spaceman’s proper time. For each leg,

Δ⁢τ=Δ⁢tγ,γ=11−v2/c2.

For leg 1:

v1=13⁢c,γ1=11−1/9=18/9=38.

Thus

τ1=6γ1=6⁢83=4⁢2≈5.66⁢yr.

For leg 2:

v2=23⁢c,γ2=11−4/9=15/9=35.

Thus

τ2=3γ2=3⁢53=5≈2.24⁢yr.

Total aging:

τ=τ1+τ2=4⁢2+5≈5.66+2.24≈7.89⁢yr.
The spaceman ages ≈7.89years.

Question 6

(a)

According to general relativity, an inertial observer is a local freely falling observer. The scientist on the space station is in orbit, so they are freely falling around Earth. Therefore the space station scientist is locally inertial.

The scientist in the Earth lab is not freely falling. The floor pushes upward on them, so they are not inertial in the GR sense.

The space station scientist is locally inertial.
The Earth lab scientist is not inertial.

This does not mean there is no gravity at the space station. It means the space station and everything inside it are freely falling together.

(b) Two circular orbits with R2>R1

For a circular orbit,

G⁢M⁢mR2=m⁢v2R.

Cancel m:

G⁢MR2=v2R.

So

v=G⁢MR.

If

R2>R1,

then

v2<v1.

The Lorentz factor is

γ=11−v2/c2.

Since the inner orbit has the larger speed,

γ1>γ2.

For clocks,

Δ⁢τ=Δ⁢tγ.

Thus the faster inner orbit clock accumulates less proper time from the special-relativistic speed effect.

Also, R2 is farther from Earth, so it is higher in the gravitational potential. Clocks higher in a gravitational field run faster. Therefore both effects point the same way: the clock at R2 runs fastest.

The scientist at ⁢R2⁢ has the fastest clock.
